# DurDir size sweep on micro-VM — baseline 2026-08-26

Actor suspend/resume latency as a function of DurableDir volume size, on the
micro-VM sandbox class (Kata Containers + Cloud Hypervisor + virtiofsd).

Measured on branch `durdir-large-sizes` at commit `bd5eea43`, which adds the
500 MiB and 1 GiB steps to the sweep. The 5 / 10 / 64 MiB steps predate it.

To repeat this measurement, read [REPRODUCING.md](REPRODUCING.md).

## What the numbers say

**Resume is floor-dominated below ~64 MiB.** `ResumeActor` moves only 1.1 s →
1.4 s across a 13× size increase from 5 to 64 MiB, then goes linear at roughly
4 ms/MiB. Back-extrapolating the linear region puts the fixed floor at ~1.15 s,
which agrees with the 5 MiB measurement.

The atelet-side logs decompose that floor. At 500 MiB the ateom cold-boot
restore is only **1.21 s** end to end, of which the VMM launch is ~843 ms
(644 ms waiting for the kata-agent to answer, 63-75 ms container setup, 124 ms
to `readyz`). So the client-observed 2.7 s at 500 MiB is mostly snapshot
download from GCS, and the micro-VM boot is a roughly constant ~0.85 s that
does not grow with volume size.

**Suspend behaves the opposite way** — a small floor (390 ms at 5 MiB) and
about double resume's marginal cost, ~7-11 ms/MiB. It is ~3× *faster* than
resume at 5 MiB and ~2× *slower* at 1 GiB; the two cross at 64 MiB, where both
sit at 1 400 ms. The asymmetry is tar-and-upload versus download-and-extract:
at 500 MiB the checkpoint spends 3.15 s in `durable_dir` alone, against 110 ms
of teardown and 2.2 ms of pause.

**Practical read:** below ~64 MiB, volume size is nearly free on resume and you
are bounded by boot. Above it, each doubling of the volume roughly doubles both
operations.

`DurDirServeWarm` tracks volume size almost perfectly linearly across the whole
range (30 / 49 / 270 / 2 100 / 4 200 ms), which is the expected shape for a
write-through virtiofs read of the whole file and a useful sanity check that
the sweep is measuring what it intends to.

## Caveats

Read these before quoting the table.

- **The large-size tails are not real percentiles.** At n=10-13, p95 is simply
  the slowest sample — p95, p99 and p100 are identical in every 500 MiB and
  1 GiB row. Only the 5 and 10 MiB rows (n≈100) support a meaningful p95. The
  committed durations (20m / 30m) are sized to give roughly n=50-60 at the top
  end; these runs were shortened to 5m / 8m while iterating on harness
  problems.
- **Locust rounds to about two significant figures** at these magnitudes, so
  "12 000" spans roughly 11 500-12 500. At the 1 GiB end that quantization is
  comparable to the p50→p95 gap itself.
- **The 5 / 10 / 64 MiB rows were measured through the gvisor scenario
  entries.** Every row in the tables above ran on micro-VM — for the 64 MiB
  run the worker logged 55 `Micro-VM booted` and 55 cold-boot restores against
  a table row of n=55, one VMM launch per measured resume — but at the time
  only `durdir_size_500mb_microvm` and `durdir_size_1gb_microvm` existed. The
  small steps were taken by pointing the gvisor entries at a workload set
  deployed with `--sandbox-class microvm`, because sandbox class is decided at
  workload-deploy time, not by the scenario. Their flags are otherwise
  identical, so the rows are sound and comparable. `durdir_size_5mb_microvm`,
  `durdir_size_10mb_microvm` and `durdir_size_64mb_microvm` have since been
  committed, so a re-run regenerates these rows from the suite — at their own
  durations (3m / 3m / 5m), which will give different sample counts than the
  5m used here.
- **The 1 GiB numbers come from a run with Envoy's route timeout raised.** The
  preceding attempt on a default 10 s timeout failed 23/23 writes with HTTP 504
  and was discarded; the row above is from the clean re-run at
  `--route-timeout=5m`. The measurement is valid for a cluster configured that
  way, which is what [REPRODUCING.md](REPRODUCING.md) step 5 sets up. See
  [Known harness limits](#known-harness-limits-found-while-producing-this).
- **No gvisor comparison was run.** `durdir_size_500mb` and `durdir_size_1gb`
  (the gvisor scenarios added in the same commit) are untested. Nothing here
  quantifies the micro-VM boot penalty relative to gvisor.

## Known harness limits found while producing this

Two of these bite only at large volume sizes and are worth knowing before the
next run.

1. **Envoy's 10 s route timeout 504s every 1 GiB write.** `defaultRouteTimeout`
   is 10 s (`cmd/atenet/internal/router/xds.go`); a 1 GiB `DurDirWrite` takes
   ~16 s and `DurDirOverwrite` ~11 s. The router accepts `--route-timeout`, and
   `manifests/ate-install/atenet-router.yaml` documents it for exactly this
   case, but `hack/install-ate.sh` has no passthrough for it — so the per-test
   `ateArgs` hook in `tests.yaml` cannot express the requirement. These runs
   used an imperative `kubectl patch` setting `--route-timeout=5m`, which does
   not survive the orchestrator's teardown/redeploy cycle. **As committed, the
   1 GiB scenarios will fail on a default cluster.**

2. **A leaked worker assignment starves the next run.** A worker hosts exactly
   one actor — `cmd/ateapi/internal/scheduling/scheduling.go` treats any worker
   with a non-nil `Status.Assignment` as busy. A run that exits before deleting
   its actor leaves the assignment behind, and with `workerCount: 1` the next
   run then fails every iteration with `ResourceExhausted: no free workers
   available`. The orchestrator redeploys workloads per test and never sees
   this; running scenarios back to back against one deployment does. Recycle
   the WorkerPool between runs.
